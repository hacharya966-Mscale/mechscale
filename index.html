Curriculum Structure

The Mechscale Learning Path

Skills in CAD compound — a weak foundation makes everything harder later. The Mechscale curriculum is sequenced so each stage genuinely prepares you for the next.

Mechanical Track

Mechanical CAD — Stage by Stage

Six stages, beginner to industry-ready. Total duration: 3–6 months depending on pace and prior background.

01

Drawing Standards & Technical Communication

Before touching CAD software, you learn how engineers communicate on paper: projection types, dimension standards, tolerancing, GD&T symbols, and title block conventions. This module is the reason Mechscale graduates can actually read drawings handed to them at job sites.

2 weeks
02

AutoCAD 2D — Production Drafting

Command-line fluency, precision drafting, layer management, block libraries, layout tabs, and printing to scale. You'll produce full drawing sets that meet ISO and IS drawing standards — the kind a manufacturer can actually use.

4–5 weeks
03

SolidWorks Parametric Modelling

Feature-based solid modelling, sketch constraints, design intent, part modifications. You'll understand why parametric history matters and how to build models that survive design changes without breaking.

6–8 weeks
04

Assembly Design & Documentation

Multi-body assemblies, mates, motion, BOMs, and exploded views. This is where part design becomes product design — understanding how components fit, move, and are manufactured together.

3–4 weeks
05

Domain Software — Fusion 360 / CATIA / Creo

Based on your target industry, you'll specialise in Fusion 360 for product design and startups, CATIA V5 for aerospace and automotive, or Creo/NX for heavy manufacturing and defence. One deep, one surveyed.

4–6 weeks
06

Industry Project & Portfolio

You'll complete a real-world component or product design brief — something with actual manufacturing constraints, not a tutorial exercise. The output is a professional portfolio piece with drawings, renders, and a BOM.

3–4 weeks
Architectural Track

Architectural CAD — Stage by Stage

Five stages from 2D plans to photorealistic renders. Duration: 3–5 months.

01

AutoCAD 2D — Architectural Drafting

Floor plans, sections, elevations, detail drawings — to IS and NBC conventions. Layer naming, annotation, and printing standards used by actual architectural offices.

4–5 weeks
02

SketchUp 3D Modelling

Rapid concept modelling, massing studies, site context, and presentation models. Push-pull geometry, component libraries, and client-presentation layouts.

3 weeks
03

3ds Max — Environment & Interior Modelling

High-detail modelling for interiors and architecture: furniture, materials, lighting rigs, and scene setup. The environment you'll render in V-Ray lives here.

5–6 weeks
04

V-Ray & Corona Rendering

Physically based rendering, HDRI lighting, material creation, camera work, and render settings. You'll produce stills that clients can't tell from photography.

4 weeks
05

Enscape Real-time Walkthroughs & Portfolio

Real-time VR-ready walkthroughs for client presentations. Final project: a complete residential or commercial design package — plans, renders, and walkthrough.

3 weeks

Who teaches at Mechscale

Hardik Acharya is a Mechanical Design Professional with over 12 years working inside engineering design projects — not teaching theory on the side, but doing the actual work. He founded Mechscale after repeatedly seeing new hires who knew the software interface but couldn't build a manufacturable model or read a shop drawing correctly.

Why Mechscale exists

Most CAD training centres teach software tutorials. Click here, type this command, done. Mechscale teaches why a feature tree should be structured a certain way, what drawing standard applies when, why that assembly mate will fail under real tolerances.

The difference shows immediately when students start working. They don't just know the tool — they know how to think with it.
